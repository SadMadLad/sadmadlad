Face/Off (1997): Movie Review

An all-time classic of Nicholas Cage and John Travolta

Note: The article includes major spoilers. If you are planning to watch the movie, I highly recommend watching the movie first.

Face/Off is an action/thriller movie featuring Nicholas Cage and John Travolta. I watched this movie for the very first time with my brother and the experience was glorius. The premise and plot is all-in and absurd, and I love it! The acting of Nick and John is amazing, the action is exciting, and all-around, it's a fun watch.

After surviving an assassination attempt from Castor Troy (played by Cage in the start) which kills his son, Sean Archer (played by Travolta early on) manages to catch Troy 6 years later. When caught, Troy hints at a time bomb he has planted with the help of his brother, Pollux Troy, a bomb which will ruin LA. Succumbing to his injuries, Castor falls into coma. Pollux affirms the bomb but does not reveal the location, and he is sent to Erehwon prison, a high-security, off-shore prison. In secrecy, Archer reluctantly agrees to go for a highly experimental face transplant from Troy in order to gather more information about the bomb from Pollux in prison. When the procedure is done, Archer is now Castor Troy in voice and appearance. He goes to the prison and tries to gather more intel about the bomb. Castor unexpectedly wakes up from his coma, assesses what has been done to him, forces the surgeon behind the transplant to plant Archer's face on him, and then kills all the officers and surgeons leaving no evidence about the whole face transplant. Now, Archer is played by Cage and Troy is played by Travolta. What follows is a chain of events full of thrill, action, identity crisis, and moral dilemma, and it will be too much for me to summarize here (so I recommend checking the movie yourself!)

Now, the premise of the movie is absurd, I get it, but it works. Once you've bought into the backstory, the movie will be an extremely exciting yet anxious watch. At times, I was rubbing my own face in anxiety, projecting what is about to happen next, like how will the bomb situation play out? How will they treat each other's loved ones? And how will their families react to them who have no idea what is going? So many questions that the movie answers in its own crazy charm. The acting of Cage and Travolta was peak. They did a fantastic job in capturing each other's mannerisms and acting. While Cage is eccentric and totally unhinged in his performance as Castor, Travolta is quiet and restrained as Archer. After the transplant, both have to match each other's energy and their initial portrayal of characters. And both did an amazing job in doing so.

Don't get me wrong, there are definitely a lot of questions that one can ask that may or may not affect their enjoyment for the movie. For example, early on, I found the whole transplant to be way out of pocket and unnecessary, thinking to myself that was this really necessary? I'm pretty sure that there were many ways FBI could've gone to detect the bomb. Secondly, why did only 4-5 people know about the face transplant? Why was there no fail-safe for FBI if anything goes wrong? Of course, these questions stuck with me in the initial scenes but I still rolled with the ludicrous nature of the operation and I am happy to say I liked the movie in its entirety.

In summary, if you want good times with this film, do not overthink it and it might be in your top 10 most favorite movies (it is in my list for sure). However, if you are nitpicky, you might get stuck on its insane plot that will hinder you from enjoying it. I personally highly recommend it! If I had to give a rating, I would give it a solid 9 out of 10. It is crazy, absurd, rewatchable and has its own charm.
